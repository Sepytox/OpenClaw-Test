The Kawasaki Legacy

Over 70 years of engineering excellence, motorsport dominance, and iconic motorcycles.

Kawasaki Heavy Industries started producing motorcycles in 1954, quickly establishing a reputation for innovation and performance. From dirt tracks to Grand Prix circuits, Kawasaki has always pushed boundaries — and we celebrate that legacy here at Vrooooom.

⚡

Raw Performance

From the supercharged Ninja H2 to the ZX-10R WSBK champion, Kawasaki builds machines that redefine what's possible on two wheels. Over 300 km/h from a production motorcycle — no other brand does it quite like this.

🏆

Motorsport Heritage

Kawasaki has accumulated dozens of World Superbike Championship titles, MotoGP victories, and off-road titles. The racetrack is the laboratory — and the results trickle down to every street bike they build.

🌍

For Every Rider

Whether you're a teenager on a KLX 110 or an experienced racer on an H2, Kawasaki has something for you. Beginner-friendly A2 models sit alongside brutal hyperbikes in the same lineup.

🔧

Engineering First

Every bolt has a reason. Every curve on the bodywork serves a purpose. Kawasaki's engineering-first philosophy means their bikes feel connected, purposeful, and precise — whether at 30 km/h in traffic or 300 km/h on a runway.

Key Milestones

1954

First motorcycle produced. Kawasaki Aircraft Co. begins manufacturing the Meihatsu motorcycle engine, laying the foundation for what would become a global brand.

1966

Kawasaki Samurai launched. The iconic A1 Samurai 250cc two-stroke twin becomes a cult classic — still admired by collectors worldwide today.

1972

Z1 changes everything. The 900cc Z1 arrives and rewrites the superbike rulebook. 82 HP, 200+ km/h — nothing like it had existed before.

1984

Ninja GPz900R debuts. The world's first true sportbike — featured in Top Gun, capable of 240 km/h. The Ninja nameplate is born.

2013

Ninja H2 announced. A supercharged production motorcycle — something previously unthinkable. 231 HP and over 300 km/h from a street-legal bike.

2015

WSBK dominance begins. The Ninja ZX-10R wins the first of many Superbike World Championship titles, cementing Kawasaki's racing pedigree.

2026

Hybrid future arrives. The Ninja 1200 Hybrid blends electric and petrol powertrains — Kawasaki enters the electrified era without compromising performance.
